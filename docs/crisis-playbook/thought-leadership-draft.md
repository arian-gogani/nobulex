## Draft (600 words)

The first major AI agent failure is not a matter of if, but when. When it happens, one question will dominate: *Can you prove what your agent did and didn't do?*

Today, most agents operate in a trust vacuum. They make API calls, execute tools, and produce outputs — but there is no cryptographic record of what was permitted, what was executed, and what was verified. When something goes wrong, we're left with logs that can be altered, claims that can't be independently verified, and a regulatory landscape that demands evidence we don't have.

That's about to change. The EU AI Act's August 2026 deadline is forcing the industry to document risk management, transparency, and human oversight. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework is pushing organizations to govern, map, measure, and manage AI risks. None of this is possible without **verifiable behavioral commitments** — covenants that agents sign, constraints that runtimes enforce, and attestations that counterparties hold.

The agent economy needs rails. Without them, every interaction is a leap of faith. With them, trust becomes a protocol: identity binding, covenant declaration, compliance proof. Three primitives. Everything else is ecosystem.

**What does "verifiable" mean in practice?** It means every agent action carries a cryptographic chain linking it back to the specific authorization that permitted it. It means counterparties hold signed records — agents can't lie about what happened because the other side has the evidence. It means canary tests actively probe covenant boundaries instead of waiting for breach. It means an evidentiary package for courts, regulators, and insurers that treats agents as accountable entities.

This isn't a training problem. It's a protocol problem. Alignment properties map to enforceable constraints. Honest behavior can be a Nash equilibrium — a computable inequality with tunable parameters. The accountability system can be accountable to itself.

The good news: the infrastructure exists. Covenants, enforcement monitors, attestation, canary testing, legal identity packages — these are not vapor. They're implemented. The path from zero to compliance is under 30 minutes: one package, one import, three lines of code to create and verify a covenant.

**Why adopt?** Not virtue — advantage. Kova-verified agents earn more: trust-gated access to premium APIs, lower insurance, marketplace priority, reduced collateral. Three wedges work without network effects: EU AI Act compliance (Aug 2026), internal agent governance, MCP server certification. The crisis becomes an accelerant, not a prerequisite.

When the first major AI agent failure hits, the organizations that can say "we can prove what our agent did and didn't do" will be the ones that survive the scrutiny. The rest will be playing catch-up.

The agent economy can't function without trust infrastructure. Verifiable behavior isn't optional — it's the floor.
