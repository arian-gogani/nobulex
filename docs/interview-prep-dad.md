## Part 1: Pre-Interview Planning

### The person I will interview is . . .
**My dad**

### What makes them interesting?
He had a hard immigration story from Iran to Sweden because of the war in Iran.

*[To be developed during interview — possible angles: resilience, identity, sacrifice, hope, family, belonging, starting over]*

---

## Interview Questions

*Remember: No yes/no questions. Be curious. Keep things comfortable. Be prepared to improvise.*

### Opening / Warm-up
1. What do you remember most vividly about the day you decided to leave Iran?
2. Walk me through what the journey from Iran to Sweden was like — not just the geography, but what was going through your mind.
3. When you first arrived in Sweden, what surprised you most — good or bad?

### The decision to leave
4. How did you and the family decide that leaving was the right thing to do?
5. What did you have to give up to make the move possible?
6. Was there a moment when you thought you might not make it?

### Building a new life
7. What was the hardest part about starting over in a new country?
8. How did you learn the language and the culture?
9. Who helped you when things were difficult?
10. What did you do to make Sweden feel like home?

### Identity and belonging
11. How do you think about your identity now — Iranian, Swedish, both, or something else?
12. What do you want your children and grandchildren to understand about where you came from?
13. If you could go back and tell your younger self one thing before the journey, what would it be?

### Reflection
14. What are you most proud of from that chapter of your life?
15. What do you wish people understood about immigrants and their stories?
16. How has that experience shaped who you are today?

---

## Notes
- Listen for specific details, names, dates, emotions
- Follow up on anything that seems important to him
- Let silence sit — don't rush to the next question
- Record (with permission) or take minimal notes so you can stay present
