## PERSONAL QUESTIONS (you write these, not me)

### "Why is this something you want to spend from 15-25 on?"

**Why I can't write this for you:** because Danielle is testing whether
this is yours or someone else's. The answer has to come from your actual
life, not a template.

**Structure to think through (NOT to say):**
1. The moment or experience that made you care about accountability.
2. Why specifically AGENTS, not just code, not just security.
3. The thing you would keep working on even if no one funded you.
4. The thing about YOU that makes this fit, not the market.

Sit with this question for an hour before the call. The honest answer
might be smaller than you think. That's fine. Small and true beats big
and rehearsed every time.

### "Tell me about yourself."

**Structure:** 60 seconds. Where you are, when you started building, what
you built before nobulex, how you got to here. Don't pitch the product.
Talk like a person.

### "Why are you the right person to build this?"

**Structure:** evidence, not adjectives. Cite things you actually did,
in chronological order. Let her draw conclusions from the evidence
rather than asserting "I'm smart."

### "What about school?"

**Structure:** must be honest. The right answer = you have a workable
plan, parents know, you're doing both. Specific is better than vague.
"I'm in [school setup], my parents know what I'm building, I work on
nobulex [X hours/days], plan is [concrete]" beats "I'll figure it out."

### "Do your parents know?"

**Required answer:** yes. If currently no, talk to them BEFORE the call.

## OBJECTIVE QUESTIONS (example answers - rewrite in your voice)

### "What does nobulex do?"

Example: "It's an open-source receipt layer for AI agents. Every action
an agent takes  - calling a tool, making a payment, reading a file  - gets
a cryptographic receipt that's signed and chained to the previous one.
Change one byte and the chain breaks. So an auditor, a customer, or a
regulator can verify what an agent actually did without trusting whoever
ran it."

Then stop. Let her ask the next question.

### "Why now?"

Example: "Two things. The EU AI Act Article 12 enforcement starts
August 2  - high-risk AI systems are required to keep tamper-evident
audit logs. Most companies deploying agents today can't produce one.
And the dollar amounts at risk are growing fast  - MiHoYo burned $300K
of AI tokens in 13 hours running agents internally. There's not enough
proof infrastructure underneath this stuff."

### "Who's the customer?"

Example: "Companies building agent infrastructure, not end users. Three
patterns I'm seeing: security platforms like Red Sentinel and AgentAudit
who need an evidence layer; trust-layer startups like t54.ai who need an
open standard underneath their product; and enterprises in regulated
industries  - finance, healthcare, defense  - who need audit trails before
regulators force them. My first integration partner is AgentAudit; I've
got a Northrop Grumman engineer who starred the repo this week."

### "How is this different from t54.ai?"

Example: "They're closed, I'm open. They raised $5M to build a trust
layer; I'm building the open-source receipt standard that runs
underneath. The historical pattern is that standards win where closed
systems can't, because adoption beats integration. They could be a
customer. We're not competitors yet, even if we look like we are."

### "If it's open source, how do you make money?"

Example: "I haven't fully figured it out, and I'm not going to pretend I
have. The hypotheses I'm testing are: hosted verification for enterprises
that don't want to run their own infra; compliance reporting that maps
receipts to specific regulatory frameworks like the EU AI Act or HIPAA;
enterprise support contracts; and possibly a governance role around the
standard itself. The wedge is the open standard; the business comes from
the things you build on top of it once everyone's using it. Right now my
job is adoption, not monetization."

### "Why solo?"

Example: "Right now, focus. I'd rather get to first paying integration
and standard validation alone than pick the wrong co-founder under time
pressure. I'm open to one if it's the right fit  - I just haven't met
that person yet."

Do NOT mention your finance friend here. Adding "but I have a friend
who might join" weakens the answer.

### "How much do you need and what for?"

Example: "$50-100K. Roughly: $15-25K for a published security audit by
a firm like Trail of Bits  - for a project positioning itself as a trust
layer, an outside cryptographic audit is the credibility unlock.
$3-5K for legal and incorporation, getting the entity set up properly.
$5-10K for travel to meet partners in person  - there are three or four
people I need to be in the same room with. $10-20K for a part-time
contractor expanding SDKs to more languages. $5-10K for infrastructure
and a runway buffer. Not a salary for me."

### "What does success look like in five years?"

Example: "Nobulex receipts are the default audit format for agent actions.
When a regulator asks an enterprise to prove what their agent did, they
hand over a receipt chain and it just works. The standard outlives the
company. The company makes money on the infrastructure people build on
top of it."
