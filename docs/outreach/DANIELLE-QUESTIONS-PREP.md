# Danielle Call: Likely Questions and How to Think About Answering

Read this the morning of the call. Do not memorize answers. The wrong
answer is a memorized one. Use this to think clearly under pressure.

## Her real filter (from her actual email)

> "Why is this something you want to spend from 15-25 on?"

She is not asking why the problem is interesting. She is asking whether
you will still be doing this in 5 years or whether this is a phase. The
honest answer is the right answer. Sit with the question for a day before
the call if you have not. Don't reach for grand. Reach for true.

## Likely questions, what she's really asking, how to answer

### "Tell me about yourself."
Real ask: can you talk like a person, not a pitch.
60 seconds max. Where you are, how you started building, what you shipped
before nobulex. Don't open with the product.

### "What does nobulex do?"
30 seconds. Plain English. No jargon. Then stop.
"Every AI agent action gets a cryptographic receipt. Change one byte, the
audit trail breaks. So a customer, auditor, or regulator can verify what
an agent did without trusting whoever ran it."

### "Why are you the right person to build this?"
Answer with evidence, not adjectives. You shipped working code solo. You
have an integration partner who got byte-identical receipts independently.
You're inside W3C and OWASP conversations. Pace of execution matters.

### "Why now?"
EU AI Act Article 12 enforcement August 2. Agents starting to move money
(MiHoYo $300K in 13 hours, Anthropic $965B valuation built on agents).
Three sentences max.

### "Who's the customer?"
Companies building agent infrastructure (AgentAudit, Red Sentinel, t54.ai),
not end users. Enterprises in regulated industries that need audit trails
before regulators force them. Specific. Never "everyone."

### "How is this different from t54.ai?"
She may know them. Your answer: they're a closed trust layer; you're the
open-source receipt standard underneath. Standards win adoption closed
systems can't. They could be a customer, not a competitor.

### "Why solo?"
Don't apologize. "I'm focused on first paying customer and standard
validation. Open to a co-founder if it's the right fit, not just any fit."
Do not loosely mention your friend as a co-founder. Sounds weak.

### "If it's open source, how do you make money?"
Honest: not figured out yet, here are hypotheses. Hosted verification
service. Compliance reporting mapped to specific regs. Enterprise support.
Possibly a governance role around the standard itself. The mistake is
pretending you have it nailed. She'll see through that.

### "How much do you need and what for?"
$50-100K. Audit ($15-25K), legal/incorporation ($3-5K), travel to meet
partners ($5-10K), part-time contractor ($10-20K), infra/runway ($5-10K).
Don't ask for $400K. She'll know you don't know what to do with it.

### "What about school?"
Workable plan, not dropping out. Parents know what you're building. Doing
it in parallel. Specific is better than vague.

### "Have your parents seen any of this?"
Right answer: yes, you've talked about what funding would mean. If you
haven't, do it before the call. A 15yo taking VC money without parents
involved is a non-starter, and possibly a legal issue.

## What NOT to say (walk back if she probes)

These were in your earlier outreach. They were tighter than the truth.

- "Microsoft merged it into AutoGen" → was a self-added ADOPTERS line
- "OWASP references it" → PR still under review, razashariff leads it now
- "10 independent teams validated" → AgentAudit + one cross-implementation
- "Singapore IMDA acknowledged us" → response to their mass email

If she pokes, walk back honestly. "Earlier framing was looser than it
should have been; here's what's actually true." Honesty about a prior
overclaim beats doubling down. She'll respect the recalibration.

## Questions to ASK her (matters as much as your answers)

1. "What do you think is the hardest thing about being 15 doing this, and
   how should I plan for it?"
2. "What milestones would make a follow-on conversation easy in 6 months?"
3. "Who else have you backed where the story rhymed with this one, and
   what did they get right or wrong?"

These signal you're thinking about the next 5 years, not just the call.

## The overall principle

She's not testing pitch polish. She's testing authenticity, commitment,
and clarity of thought. The 0% AI email you wrote her is what got you
here. Bring that same person to the call.

## Logistics

- Take it on Zoom unless you can fly. Either is fine.
- Quiet room, decent lighting, headphones with mic.
- Have a notebook open. Take notes during the call; she'll notice.
- 30 minutes is enough. Don't overstay.
- Within 24 hours: send a 4-line follow-up email referencing one specific
  thing she said + one concrete next step. In your own voice.
